## SECTION 5: THE SCIENCE

### English

**Headline:** Open methodology. Not a black box.

**Body:**
PeakAerobic is built on published, peer-reviewed science — not proprietary algorithms with hidden assumptions. Every threshold you see can be traced to a specific method and a specific paper.

**Threshold detection** uses three independent approaches:
- **ModDmax** (Bishop, Jenkins & Mackinnon, 1998) — the modified Dmax method, which finds the point of maximum perpendicular distance from the lactate curve to a reference line starting at the first significant lactate rise.
- **Baseline Rise** (Faude, Kindermann & Meyer, 2009) — identifies LT1 as baseline + 0.5 mmol/L and LT2 as the first sustained rise above 3.1 mmol/L, calibrated against Faude's meta-analysis.
- **Sustained Increase** — detects the first point where lactate rises and does not return, confirmed by subsequent data points.

All three methods run independently. Results are aggregated using the median for pace/power/HR (ensuring the reported value matches an actual test stage) and the mean for lactate concentration.

**Physiological modeling:**
- **VLamax** is estimated from the LT1/LT2 ratio as a proxy for glycolytic capacity, following Mader's (2003) framework.
- **VO2max** is estimated using the Swain & ACSM heart-rate-based formula when gas exchange data is unavailable.
- **Race prediction** uses di Prampero's energy cost model, which accounts for aerobic power, anaerobic capacity, and running economy.

**Periodization** follows Jan Olbrecht's block model from *The Science of Winning*, selecting from 6 block types based on your aerobic/anaerobic capacity profile, phase of season, and gap analysis.

**Key references:**
- Bishop, D., Jenkins, D.G., & Mackinnon, L.T. (1998). The relationship between plasma lactate parameters, Wpeak and 1-h cycling performance in women. *Medicine and Science in Sports and Exercise*, 30(8), 1270-1275.
- Faude, O., Kindermann, W., & Meyer, T. (2009). Lactate threshold concepts. *Sports Medicine*, 39(6), 469-490.
- Mader, A. (2003). Glycolysis and oxidative phosphorylation as a function of cytoplasmic phosphorylation state and power output of the muscle cell. *European Journal of Applied Physiology*, 88(4-5), 317-338.
- Olbrecht, J. (2000). *The Science of Winning: Planning, Periodizing and Optimizing Swim Training*. Luton, England: Swimshop.
- di Prampero, P.E. (1986). The energy cost of human locomotion on land and in water. *International Journal of Sports Medicine*, 7(2), 55-72.

## SECTION 9: FAQ

### English

**Q: What data do I need to use PeakAerobic?**
A: At minimum, you need lactate concentration (mmol/L) and the corresponding pace, power, or speed for each test stage. Heart rate is optional but enables VO2max estimation and more accurate zone calculation. Any standard incremental protocol works — 3-minute stages, 4-minute stages, Mader, Haas, custom.

**Q: How accurate is the analysis?**
A: PeakAerobic runs three peer-reviewed detection methods (ModDmax, baseline rise, sustained increase) and cross-validates them. Each threshold receives a confidence score from 0 to 100%. When methods disagree significantly, the score drops and you are warned. In our internal validation against manual expert analysis, agreement exceeds 90% for well-conducted tests.

**Q: Do I need a lactate meter?**
A: Yes — PeakAerobic analyzes lactate test data, so you need test results to input. Any portable lactate analyzer (Lactate Pro 2, Lactate Scout, EKF Biosen) or lab-grade device works. We do not generate lactate values; we interpret yours.

**Q: Is it really free?**
A: The Free tier gives you the full analysis engine for 1 athlete and 3 tests — no feature gating, no watermarks, no trial expiration. It is free because we believe access to good science should not require a subscription. Coaches and labs who need unlimited athletes and periodization tools can upgrade to Pro.

**Q: Can I use it for cycling, swimming, and triathlon?**
A: Yes. PeakAerobic supports running (pace-based), cycling (power-based), and swimming (pace-based). Each discipline has its own threshold benchmarks, workout families, and dose ladders. Triathlon athletes get a unified view across all three.

**Q: How is this different from INSCYD?**
A: Three key differences. First, transparency: PeakAerobic shows you the methods, the math, and the confidence — INSCYD is a black box. Second, threshold detection: we use 3 independent methods with cross-validation; INSCYD uses a single proprietary model. Third, price: our full engine is free for individual use. INSCYD starts at EUR49/month plus per-athlete fees.

**Q: Do you store my data?**
A: Yes, your data is stored securely so you can track changes across tests over time. We do not sell, share, or use your data for any purpose other than providing you the service. You can delete your account and all associated data at any time.

**Q: Can I export reports?**
A: Pro and Team plans include PDF report export with full analysis, charts, and training recommendations. Free tier users can screenshot or print the web view.

**Q: What protocols are supported?**
A: Any incremental step protocol. The engine adapts to 3-minute, 4-minute, 5-minute, or custom stage durations. It also handles non-uniform protocols where stage duration varies. The minimum requirement is 4 stages with lactate values; 6 or more stages are recommended for high-confidence results.

**Q: Can I import data from other platforms?**
A: You can paste data from any spreadsheet or upload a CSV file. We are working on direct integrations with popular testing platforms. If you have a specific format, contact us — we are happy to add parsers.

**Preview text:** Your thresholds tell you WHERE you are. VLamax tells you WHY.

---

Hi {{first_name}},

You have your thresholds. Good. But there's a number that most coaches never see — and it explains *why* two athletes with the same LT2 need completely different training.

It's called **VLamax**: your maximum lactate production rate.

Think of it as a two-tank model:

- **Tank 1 (aerobic):** Slow, efficient, nearly unlimited fuel. Your VO2max determines its size.
- **Tank 2 (glycolytic):** Fast, powerful, burns through quickly. VLamax determines its size.

A high VLamax means your glycolytic engine is dominant — great for sprinters, problematic for endurance athletes. A low VLamax means you're efficient but may lack top-end power.

PeakAerobic estimates your VLamax from your lactate curve and shows you where you sit: low, moderate, or high. This single number determines whether you need more base work or more intensity.

## Email 4 — Differentiation (Day 5)

### ENGLISH

**Subject:** The 4mmol myth is costing your athletes fitness

**Preview text:** Fixed thresholds were debunked in 1981. Here's what to use instead.

---

Hi {{first_name}},

Most lactate analysis tools draw a horizontal line at 4mmol and call it your threshold. That method — the Mader fixed threshold — was proposed in 1976 and challenged by the same research community within five years.

Here's the problem: **individual lactate curves vary enormously.**

Some athletes hit their real LT2 at 2.8mmol. Others at 5.2mmol. Using 4mmol for both means one trains too hard and the other too easy. Neither improves optimally.

PeakAerobic uses three independent detection methods — baseline rise, sustained increase, and modified Dmax (Bishop 1998) — then cross-validates them. You get the threshold that matches *your* athlete's physiology, not a population average from the 1970s.

The difference in practice? Training zones that actually correspond to metabolic reality. Prescribed intensities that drive adaptation instead of accumulating unnecessary fatigue.

I wrote a full breakdown of the science if you want to go deeper.

## Sequence suppression rules
- If user starts Pro trial, suppress remaining onboarding emails (5, 6, 7)

- If user unsubscribes from marketing, suppress win-back and re-engagement but keep transactional (trial expiry notice)
- Re-engagement sequence: max 1 email per 30-day inactivity window, do not repeat within 90 days

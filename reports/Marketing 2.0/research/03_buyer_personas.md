# PeakAerobic Buyer Personas

Research-backed profiles of the five core user segments for PeakAerobic, based on web research across coaching forums (Slowtwitch, TrainerRoad, LetsRun), industry reports (USA Triathlon State of the Sport), academic literature, and product/pricing pages (INSCYD, TrainingPeaks, WKO5, Intervals.icu).

---

## Persona 1: The Independent Coach — "Marco"

### Summary
Freelance endurance coach running a one-person coaching business. Manages 12-25 athletes across triathlon, running, or cycling. Charges $100-200/month per athlete. Uses TrainingPeaks as primary platform. Wants to offer lactate testing but finds INSCYD too expensive for the volume of tests he runs.

### Demographics
- **Age:** 32-48
- **Gender:** 70% male (mirrors USAT coaching demographics)
- **Income:** $40,000-80,000/year from coaching (often supplemented by a part-time job or partner income). At 20 athletes x $150/month = $36,000 gross before tools and taxes
- **Geography:** English-speaking markets (US, UK, Australia, Canada), plus strong pockets in Spain, Germany, Netherlands
- **Education:** Sports science degree or coaching certification (USAT Level I/II, TrainingPeaks Certified Coach, NSCA-CSCS). Some self-taught ex-athletes

### How Many Athletes They Manage
- **Starting out:** 1-5 athletes (often part-time, still has a day job)
- **Established full-time:** 15-25 athletes (Slowtwitch forum consensus: "20 is comfortable, 30 is burning out")
- **High-volume model:** 40-80 athletes (template-heavy, lower per-athlete attention; one Slowtwitch thread flagged "how can a coach with 80 athletes on the roster give quality attention?")
- **Sweet spot for PeakAerobic:** The 10-25 range -- big enough to need tools, small enough that INSCYD's per-test cost stings

### Budget for Tools
| Tool | Monthly Cost | Role |
|------|-------------|------|
| TrainingPeaks Coach Edition | $22/month (rising to $55 for Unlimited) | Plan delivery, calendar, TSS tracking |
| INSCYD (if used) | From EUR 49/month + per-test fees | Metabolic profiling, VLamax |
| Lactate Pro 2 or Lactate Scout 4 | $300-500 device + $3.50/test strip | Field testing |
| WKO5 | $169 one-time | Power curve analysis |
| Intervals.icu | Free | Supplementary analysis |
| Zoom/Google Meet | Free-$15/month | Athlete check-ins |
| **Total tool budget:** | **$50-150/month** (excluding hardware) | |

They are price-sensitive on subscriptions because margins are thin. A free tool that replaces a $50+/month line item is immediately attractive.

### Technical Sophistication
- Comfortable with TrainingPeaks, Garmin Connect, Strava dashboards
- Understands TSS, CTL/ATL, FTP, lactate curves conceptually
- May struggle with deeper physiological modeling (VO2max estimation, VLamax, fat oxidation rates)
- Wants software to do the math so they can focus on coaching decisions
- NOT developers -- needs clean UI, not spreadsheets

### Where They Hang Out Online
- **Forums:** Slowtwitch (triathlon), TrainerRoad Forum (cycling), LetsRun (running)
- **Podcasts:** That Triathlon Show (Mikael Eriksson), Ask a Cycling Coach (TrainerRoad), Fast Talk (Fast Talk Labs), The Endurance Coachpreneur (Michelle Lake)
- **Social media:** Instagram (posting athlete results, race recaps), YouTube (coaching content, product reviews)
- **Professional:** TrainingPeaks Coach Blog, INSCYD webinars, NSCA conferences
- **Accounts they follow:** @scientifictriathlon, @inscyd_official, @trainingpeaks, @fasttalklabs, Dan Lorang, Sebastian Weber, Tim Cusick (WKO)

### What Keeps Them Up at Night
1. **"Am I charging enough?"** -- Thin margins at $150/month with 15 athletes. One lost athlete = real income hit
2. **"How do I differentiate from every other coach on TrainingPeaks?"** -- Hundreds of certified coaches competing for the same age-group market
3. **"I bought a Lactate Pro 2 but I don't know how to interpret the data beyond 2mmol/4mmol"** -- Paid $400 for hardware, still eyeballing curves on a spreadsheet
4. **"INSCYD would make me look legit but EUR 49/month plus per-test fees eats my margins"** -- Wants the metabolic profiling cachet without the subscription cost
5. **"I lose athletes every winter because they don't see enough value"** -- Needs tangible deliverables (reports, charts, progress tracking) to justify monthly fee

### Exact Language They Use
- "I need something that makes my testing more professional without breaking the bank"
- "My athletes ask me about VLamax and I don't have a tool to show them"
- "I can't justify INSCYD's price for the 3-4 tests I do per month"
- "I want to offer lab-quality analysis from a field test"
- "TrainingPeaks is great for planning but terrible for lactate analysis"

### Purchase Triggers for PeakAerobic
- Sees a demo showing automated LT1/LT2 detection with professional-looking reports
- A respected coach or podcast guest mentions the tool
- Realizes PeakAerobic is free and does VLamax estimation -- a feature locked behind INSCYD's paywall
- Loses an athlete to a competitor who offers "metabolic profiling"

### Objections
- "Is the science actually valid, or is this a toy?"
- "Can I brand these reports with my logo?"
- "Will it integrate with TrainingPeaks or do I have to double-enter data?"
- "If it's free, what's the catch? Will it suddenly become paid?"
- "My athletes' data -- is it private?"

## Persona 2: The Self-Coached Age Grouper -- "Sarah"

### Summary
40-year-old triathlete training for her third 70.3. Has a Garmin Forerunner 265, Strava Premium, and follows TrainingPeaks plans she buys from the marketplace ($50-100 one-time). Considered hiring a coach but can't justify $150+/month. Recently bought a Lactate Scout after hearing about lactate testing on a podcast. Has no idea what to do with the data.

### Demographics
- **Age:** 35-52 (median age of triathletes is 38-40)
- **Gender:** 40% female, 60% male (USAT membership split: 59.6% male, 39.5% female)
- **Income:** Household income $126,000-$247,000 (USAT average $126k; Ironman participants $247k)
- **Annual spend on triathlon:** $2,865-$5,631 (USA Triathlon 2024 State of the Sport)
- **Geography:** US suburbs (especially Colorado, California, Texas, Florida), UK, Australia, Germany
- **Career:** Professional -- engineer, doctor, lawyer, tech worker, business owner

### Why They Don't Have a Coach
Only 9% of triathletes use a coach. The other 91% cite:
1. **Cost:** $150-250/month feels steep when they already spend $5,000+/year on gear, races, and nutrition
2. **Independence:** "I enjoy figuring out training myself. It's part of the hobby"
3. **Schedule flexibility:** "With my job and two kids, I need to move workouts around constantly. A rigid plan from a coach doesn't work"
4. **Past bad experience:** "I had a coach for 6 months and got a cookie-cutter plan I could've downloaded for free"
5. **Ego:** "I've read enough books and podcasts to do this myself" (overconfidence in self-knowledge)

### What Tools They Currently Use
| Tool | How They Use It |
|------|----------------|
| Garmin watch (Forerunner 265/965, Fenix 7/8) | Records every session; obsesses over Training Status and VO2max estimate |
| Strava (Premium/Summit) | Social validation, Relative Effort, route planning, Athlete Intelligence (AI beta) |
| TrainingPeaks (free or purchased plan) | Follows a marketplace plan; rarely uses the analytics features |
| Intervals.icu | Discovered via a forum; uses for CTL/ATL charts because it's free |
| Spreadsheet / Notes app | Logs subjective RPE, sleep, "how legs felt" |
| Lactate Scout or Lactate Pro 2 (if bought) | Tests occasionally, photographs the strip, types results into a spreadsheet |

### Their Relationship with Data
- **Collects everything, analyzes little.** Has 3 years of Garmin data, never exported a CSV
- **Trusts black-box metrics** (Garmin VO2max, Training Status) but gets frustrated when they seem wrong ("Garmin says unproductive but I just set a 10k PR")
- **Understands zones conceptually** (Zone 2 = easy, Zone 4 = threshold) but doesn't know how they're calculated
- **Craves depth but lacks tools.** Wants to know "why did my pace drop at the same heart rate?" but doesn't know how to investigate
- **Visual learner.** A chart showing LT1/LT2 progression over time would be instantly compelling

### What Would Make Them Pay for Coaching
- A race-specific plan (e.g., "Ironman 70.3 in 12 weeks") with lactate-based zone prescription
- A coach who offers testing + interpretation as a package (not just a training plan)
- Reaching a plateau and failing to improve despite training more
- An injury scare that makes them realize they need periodization guidance

### Exact Language They Use
- "I know I should probably get a coach but $200/month for someone to tell me to run easy is a lot"
- "I did a lactate test at a local lab and got a PDF with numbers I don't understand"
- "My Garmin says my VO2max is 48 but I don't know if that's good for my age"
- "I want to train smarter, not just harder"
- "Where do I even enter lactate data? There's no app for this"

### Purchase Triggers for PeakAerobic
- Buys a portable lactate meter and immediately realizes there's no consumer-friendly app to log and analyze results
- Hears about VLamax on That Triathlon Show and wants to estimate hers without paying $200+ for an INSCYD test
- Searches "free lactate analysis tool" or "how to calculate lactate threshold from test data"
- A friend in her tri club shares a PeakAerobic report screenshot on Instagram

### Objections
- "I don't do lactate tests -- is this still useful for me?" (needs HR/pace-only onramp)
- "Will this replace TrainingPeaks?" (doesn't want another subscription/another app)
- "This looks too scientific for me -- is it for coaches or athletes?"
- "I don't want to prick my finger"

## Persona 3: The Sports Science Student/Graduate -- "Alex"

### Summary
25-year-old recent Exercise Science graduate working part-time as a personal trainer while looking for a full-time S&C or coaching role. Has strong theoretical knowledge of lactate kinetics, VO2max, and periodization but has never worked with real athlete data at scale. Sees PeakAerobic as both a learning tool and a portfolio builder.

### Demographics
- **Age:** 21-28
- **Gender:** Roughly balanced (sports science enrollment is ~55% male, 45% female)
- **Income:** $25,000-45,000 (part-time PT work, possibly still studying). Broke but investing in career
- **Geography:** Global -- US, UK, Australia, Spain, Germany, Scandinavia, Netherlands have strong sports science programs
- **Education:** BSc or MSc in Exercise Science, Sports Science, Kinesiology, Human Movement

### Knowledge Level
- **Theoretical: 8/10.** Can explain the Mader lactate shuttle model, Olbrecht's training theory, knows the difference between LT1 and LT2, understands VLamax conceptually
- **Practical: 3/10.** Has done 2-3 lab practicals during their degree, never run an independent lactate test in the field, never designed a periodized plan for a real athlete
- **Software: 5/10.** Comfortable with Excel/SPSS for university assignments, may know R or Python basics, but hasn't used commercial coaching tools
- **Coaching: 2/10.** Has coached friends informally, maybe assisted a university team, but no paid roster of athletes

### Job Market Reality
- 78% of sports science graduates with specialized concentrations find roles within 6 months, but many start part-time
- S&C jobs are growing ~10-20% through 2028, salaries $54,000-$83,400
- 65% of sports organizations plan to increase AI use, creating a skills gap that Alex wants to fill
- Key frustration: "I have a degree but no portfolio of athlete work to show employers"

### Why They'd Use PeakAerobic
1. **Portfolio building:** "I can run field tests on my training partners and generate professional reports to show potential employers"
2. **Bridge theory to practice:** "I studied the Mader model in class -- now I can actually see VLamax estimated from real data"
3. **Differentiation:** "Other graduates just have a degree. I can show I've done metabolic profiling on 10 athletes"
4. **Cost:** "INSCYD costs EUR 49/month minimum and I'm making $20/hour as a PT. PeakAerobic is free"
5. **Credibility proof:** "When I pitch myself to a cycling team, I can show them example reports"

### Career Aspirations PA Helps With
- **Short-term (0-2 years):** Build a small roster of 3-5 athletes to coach, using PA for testing and reporting
- **Medium-term (2-5 years):** Get hired by a sports institute, university, or pro team as a physiologist/performance analyst
- **Long-term (5+ years):** Run own coaching practice or performance lab with full physiological profiling capability

### Media Consumption
- **YouTube:** Andy Galpin, Stronger by Science, Olbrecht lectures, INSCYD masterclasses (free ones)
- **Podcasts:** Sigma Nutrition Radio, Endure IQ, Fast Talk Labs, That Triathlon Show
- **Academic:** PubMed, ResearchGate, follows key researchers (Faude, Seiler, Laursen)
- **Social media:** Twitter/X for sports science debates, Instagram for coaching content
- **Books:** Olbrecht "The Science of Winning," Daniels "Running Formula," Coggan "Training and Racing with a Power Meter"

### Exact Language They Use
- "I know the theory but I've never actually analyzed real lactate data outside a lab practical"
- "I need something on my CV that proves I can do metabolic profiling"
- "INSCYD is the gold standard but there's no way I can afford it right now"
- "I want to start coaching but I don't have the tools to look professional"
- "Where can I practice interpreting test results without access to a lab?"

### Purchase Triggers for PeakAerobic
- Professor or mentor recommends it as a practical learning tool
- Finds it while searching "free lactate analysis software" or "VLamax estimation tool"
- Sees a LinkedIn post from another graduate showing PA-generated reports
- Needs a portfolio piece for a job application

### Objections
- "Is the methodology peer-reviewed?" (will scrutinize the science more than any other persona)
- "Does it use ModDmax or classical Dmax? Which threshold detection method?" (wants to know the exact algorithm)
- "Can I export the raw data for my own analysis?"
- "Will employers take results from a free tool seriously?"

## Persona 4: The Data-Obsessed Athlete -- "Javier"

### Summary
34-year-old competitive amateur cyclist who trains 12-16 hours/week. Has a Garmin Edge 1040, power meter, HR strap, and accounts on Strava, Intervals.icu, and TrainingPeaks. Follows every cycling metric podcast. Frustrated that Garmin's VO2max estimate "makes no sense" and wants deeper physiological insight. Would absolutely share a VLamax chart on Instagram if it made him look fast.

### Demographics
- **Age:** 28-42
- **Gender:** 80% male (cycling skews heavily male in the data-obsessed segment)
- **Income:** $60,000-$120,000 (has disposable income for tech but is value-conscious)
- **Geography:** US, Europe (Spain, Italy, Belgium, Netherlands, UK), Australia
- **Sport:** Primarily cycling, some running or triathlon. Races crits, gran fondos, or local TTs. Might do a sportive/fondo

### Current Tool Stack
| Tool | Monthly Cost | Satisfaction |
|------|-------------|-------------|
| Strava Premium | $12/month | Loves the social, wishes the analysis was deeper |
| Intervals.icu | Free | "Best free tool out there" -- uses daily for PMC, power curve, TRIMP |
| TrainingPeaks (Premium or free) | $0-$12/month | Uses occasionally for structured workouts |
| Garmin Connect | Free | Trusts GPS data, distrusts computed metrics |
| WKO5 (maybe) | $169 one-time | "I bought it but it's overwhelming" |
| TrainerRoad (maybe) | $20/month | Indoor training, AI FTP detection |
| GoldenCheetah (maybe) | Free | "Tried it, too ugly, gave up" |

### Wants More Depth But Doesn't Know Where to Get It
- Knows his FTP is 280W but wants to know WHY he fades in 40km TTs
- Has heard about VLamax, fat oxidation zones, and carb combustion rates on podcasts but has no way to measure or estimate them
- Frustrated by Garmin's black-box metrics: "Garmin says my VO2max is 50.5, Intervals.icu says 46, which one is right?"
- Wants to understand his lactate curve but doesn't want to pay $200+ for a lab test
- Would buy a Lactate Scout if someone told him what to do with the data

### Social Media Behavior
- Posts Strava ride screenshots on Instagram stories
- Shares power PRs, FTP tests, and race results
- Would 100% share a VLamax/VO2max chart or lactate curve if it looked clean and professional
- Follows cycling data influencers: @the5krunner, @gaborherczig (WKO), @inscyd_official, @dcrainmaker
- Active on TrainerRoad Forum, r/cycling, r/Velo

### Exact Language They Use
- "Garmin's Training Status is worthless -- it says I'm unproductive when I just set a PR"
- "I want lab-level data without going to a lab"
- "Intervals.icu is incredible for free but it doesn't do metabolic profiling"
- "I've been hearing about VLamax everywhere but there's no free way to estimate it"
- "I bought WKO5 and felt like I needed a PhD to use it"

### Psychographics
- **Values:** Precision, optimization, marginal gains, being the smartest person in the group ride
- **Motivations:** Beat last year's FTP, understand physiology, look knowledgeable when talking training
- **Fears:** Leaving performance on the table, training wrong, getting slower with age
- **Identity:** "I'm not just a cyclist, I'm a student of the sport"

### Purchase Triggers for PeakAerobic
- Sees a YouTube video comparing PeakAerobic's VLamax estimate vs INSCYD results
- A cycling forum thread recommends it as "INSCYD for free"
- Wants to see what his lactate curve looks like without paying for a full lab test
- The UI produces shareable charts that look good on social media

### Objections
- "Is this accurate enough to trust for training decisions?"
- "Can I import my power/HR data from Garmin?"
- "The UI needs to be as clean as Intervals.icu or I won't use it"
- "I don't want to prick my finger -- can it work with estimated data?"
- "Why should I use this instead of just paying for one INSCYD test?"

## Persona 5: The Pro Team Physiologist -- "Dr. Chloe"

### Summary
31-year-old exercise physiologist with a PhD, working for a WorldTour development team / national federation / university sports performance unit. Manages testing for 10-30 athletes across multiple disciplines. Currently uses INSCYD and/or custom lab software (Cosmed, Cortex for gas exchange). Interested in PeakAerobic as a supplementary field-testing tool or a free option for satellite athletes who can't visit the lab regularly.

### Demographics
- **Age:** 28-40
- **Gender:** ~50/50 (sports science PhDs are relatively gender-balanced)
- **Income:** $45,000-$75,000 (university/institute salary; national federations pay more in some countries)
- **Geography:** UK (English Institute of Sport), Australia (AIS), Germany (Olympiastuetzpunkt), Netherlands, Scandinavia, US (USOPC), Spain (CAR), France (INSEP)
- **Education:** MSc or PhD in Exercise Physiology, Sports Science, or Human Performance

### Current Testing Workflow
| Step | Tool | Pain Point |
|------|------|-----------|
| Lab VO2max test | Cosmed/Cortex metabolic cart | $50,000+ equipment, athletes must travel to lab |
| Lactate step test | Lab-grade analyzer (YSI/Biosen) or Lactate Pro 2 | Manual data entry into Excel for analysis |
| Data analysis | Custom Excel spreadsheets / MATLAB / INSCYD | No standardized workflow; each physiologist has their own spreadsheet |
| Reporting | PowerPoint / Word doc / INSCYD PDF | Time-consuming manual report generation |
| Athlete communication | Email PDF + phone call with coach | Coach often doesn't fully understand the report |

### INSCYD Relationship
- Uses INSCYD for metabolic profiling (VO2max, VLamax, FatMax, carb combustion)
- Values the Olbrecht/Weber scientific framework
- **Pain point: cost.** At EUR 49+/month plus per-test fees, running 30 tests/month on development athletes gets expensive
- **Pain point: black box.** "I trust Sebastian Weber's model but I can't see the algorithm. I want to verify the math"
- **Pain point: lock-in.** "All my athlete data lives inside INSCYD. If I cancel, I lose access"

### Decision-Making Process
- Does NOT make purchasing decisions alone -- must justify to head coach, performance director, or budget committee
- Prioritizes tools backed by peer-reviewed methodology
- Needs to demonstrate ROI: "This tool saved X hours per testing cycle" or "This identified a threshold shift we would have missed"
- Will trial free tools but needs data security and privacy guarantees before putting athlete data in
- Influenced by what other national federations or WorldTour teams use

### Exact Language They Use
- "I need something that scales -- I can't spend 45 minutes per athlete building an Excel report"
- "INSCYD is great but the per-test cost doesn't work when I'm profiling 25 development athletes quarterly"
- "I want to see the threshold detection algorithm. Show me the methodology"
- "Can I bulk-upload lactate data from our testing sessions?"
- "The coaches don't read 10-page reports. I need a one-page visual summary they'll actually look at"
- "Data sovereignty matters -- where is athlete data stored?"

### Psychographics
- **Values:** Scientific rigor, reproducibility, efficiency, evidence-based practice
- **Motivations:** Advance athlete performance, publish research, build reputation in the field, get hired by a better program
- **Fears:** Making a training recommendation based on flawed data, losing credibility with coaches, being replaced by AI-driven tools
- **Identity:** "I'm the bridge between lab science and coaching practice"

### Purchase Triggers for PeakAerobic
- Discovers it offers ModDmax threshold detection (a method they know from Bishop 1998)
- Sees it can replace their Excel workflow for field-based lactate tests
- A colleague at another federation mentions using it for satellite athletes
- Free price point means no budget approval needed for a trial
- API or data export capability for integration with existing workflows

### Objections
- "Is the threshold detection methodology validated against gold-standard lab measurements?"
- "Where is athlete data stored? Is it GDPR-compliant?" (European federations especially)
- "Can I customize the analysis parameters (e.g., change baseline criterion from +0.5 to +0.4)?"
- "This looks like it's designed for coaches, not physiologists. Can it handle the complexity I need?"
- "I need multi-sport support (running, cycling, swimming) with discipline-specific protocols"
- "If the tool disappears tomorrow, can I export all my data?"

## Cross-Persona Comparison Matrix

| Dimension | Marco (Coach) | Sarah (Self-Coached) | Alex (Student) | Javier (Data Nerd) | Dr. Chloe (Physiologist) |
|-----------|--------------|---------------------|----------------|--------------------|-----------------------|
| **Primary need** | Differentiate business, retain athletes | Understand training data | Build portfolio | Deeper metrics | Scale testing workflow |
| **Lactate testing?** | Yes, with portable device | Maybe (just bought one or considering) | Yes, in uni lab | Curious but hasn't started | Yes, lab + field |
| **Willingness to pay** | Would pay if ROI clear ($10-30/mo) | Unlikely to pay monthly; might pay one-time | Free only (broke) | Would pay $5-15/mo for premium features | Would pay if it replaces INSCYD ($30-50/mo) |
| **Technical depth needed** | Medium (interpret, not configure) | Low (show me what to do) | High (wants to see the algorithm) | Medium-High (loves data, hates complexity) | Very High (wants configurable parameters) |
| **Key channel** | Coaching podcasts, TrainingPeaks blog | Tri club WhatsApp, Instagram | University, LinkedIn | TrainerRoad Forum, r/Velo, YouTube | Conferences (ECSS, ACSM), PubMed |
| **Biggest fear** | Losing athletes to competition | Wasting training time | Not getting hired | Training suboptimally | Making bad recommendations |
| **INSCYD relationship** | Wants it, can't afford it | Has heard of it, doesn't understand it | Studied it in class, can't access it | Curious, would try it if free | Uses it, frustrated by cost/opacity |

## Adoption Sequence

Based on research, the optimal go-to-market sequence is:

1. **Alex (Student) + Javier (Data Nerd)** -- First adopters. Zero friction (free), high engagement, will spread the word on forums and social media. Alex builds credibility content; Javier shares charts on Instagram/Strava.

2. **Marco (Coach)** -- Follows when he sees athletes or forum posts mentioning PeakAerobic. Converts when he realizes he can generate professional reports for free. Becomes a power user and potential advocate.

3. **Sarah (Self-Coached)** -- Arrives via word-of-mouth from her tri club (where Marco coaches other members) or from Javier's Instagram posts. Needs the simplest onboarding path.

4. **Dr. Chloe (Physiologist)** -- Last to adopt, highest value per user. Needs methodology documentation, data export, and privacy guarantees. Once one federation physiologist adopts, peer pressure drives others.

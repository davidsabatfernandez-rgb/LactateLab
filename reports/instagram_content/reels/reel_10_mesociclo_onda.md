# Reel 10: El principio de onda — por que no entrenas igual todas las semanas

## Concepto
Explicar el principio de variacion sistematica de carga (wave principle) dentro de un mesociclo: sobrecarga progresiva seguida de recuperacion planificada. Fundamentado en el modelo impulse-response de Banister (1991) y la teoria de bloques de Issurin (2010).

## Caption (Espanol)
La adaptacion al entrenamiento no es lineal. El principio de onda alterna semanas de sobrecarga progresiva con semanas de recuperacion planificada. Segun el modelo impulse-response de Banister (1991), el rendimiento es la diferencia entre el fitness acumulado y la fatiga residual. Sin variacion sistematica, la fatiga se acumula y el rendimiento se estanca.

"Systematic load variation is essential for continued adaptation" — Issurin (2010).

Refs:
- Banister, E.W. (1991). Modeling elite athletic performance. In: Physiological Testing of the High-Performance Athlete.
- Issurin, V.B. (2010). New horizons for the methodology and physiology of training periodization. Sports Medicine, 40(3), 189-206.

## Caption (English)
Training adaptation is not linear. The wave principle alternates progressive overload weeks with planned recovery. According to Banister's impulse-response model (1991), performance equals accumulated fitness minus residual fatigue. Without systematic variation, fatigue accumulates and performance stagnates.

Refs:
- Banister, E.W. (1991). Modeling elite athletic performance.
- Issurin, V.B. (2010). Sports Medicine, 40(3), 189-206.

## Scientific rigor notes
- Banister (1991) impulse-response model is foundational in exercise science for modeling training effects
- Issurin (2010) provides evidence for systematic load variation in block periodization
- Supercompensation is presented as a simplified model; in reality adaptation is multi-factorial
- No specific magnitude claims are made about recovery timelines

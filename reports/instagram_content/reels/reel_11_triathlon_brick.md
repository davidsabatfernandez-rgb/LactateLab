# Reel 11: El error mas comun en triathlon — economia de carrera post-ciclismo

## Concepto

## Caption (Espanol)
Correr tras el ciclismo no es lo mismo que correr en fresco. La economia de carrera puede disminuir entre un 2% y un 8% tras el segmento de bici, dependiendo de la intensidad y duracion del ciclismo previo (Millet et al., 2002). Este efecto se relaciona con la deplecion parcial de glucogeno, la acumulacion termica y la alteracion del patron motor. Las brick sessions son utiles para entrenar la transicion, pero deben planificarse con suficiente recuperacion posterior.

Refs:
- Millet, G.P. et al. (2002). Modelling the transfers of training effects on performance in elite triathletes. IJSM, 23(1), 55-63.
- Hausswirth, C. & Mujika, I. (2013). Recovery for Performance in Sport. Human Kinetics.

## Caption (English)
Running after cycling is not the same as running fresh. Running economy may decrease by approximately 2-8% after the bike segment, depending on cycling intensity and duration (Millet et al., 2002). This effect relates to partial glycogen depletion, thermal accumulation, and altered motor patterns. Brick sessions train this transition, but must be planned with adequate recovery.

Refs:
- Millet, G.P. et al. (2002). IJSM, 23(1), 55-63.
- Hausswirth, C. & Mujika, I. (2013). Recovery for Performance in Sport.

## Scientific rigor notes
- Millet et al. (2002) is the primary citation for the 2-8% running economy decrease after cycling
- Mechanisms (glycogen depletion, thermal load, motor pattern alteration) are well-established in triathlon physiology
- No absolute performance claims — only relative economy changes
